Phone Help Guides: The 4 Things People Ask About Most

Medha Holla · Customer guide · iPhone and Android

Summary. Simple step-by-step guides for the four phone jobs customers most often need help with. Each one is written so you can follow it yourself, or so I can walk a customer through it in a few minutes.

1. Setting up an eSIM · 2. Moving everything to a new phone · 3. Fixing "my mobile data isn't working" · 4. Getting your phone ready for an overseas trip

1. Setting up an eSIM

An eSIM is a digital SIM built into your phone. There's no plastic card: your carrier gives you a QR code or sets it up in their app.

Before you start

  • Make sure your phone supports eSIM (most iPhones from the XS onward and most recent Samsung and Pixel phones do).
  • Connect to Wi-Fi and have your carrier's QR code or app ready.

iPhone

  1. Open Settings → Mobile Service (on older iOS it's called Mobile Data).
  2. Tap Add eSIM → Use QR Code, and scan the code from your carrier.
  3. Follow the prompts, then label the line (for example "Personal").
  4. Test it: turn off Wi-Fi, make a call and open a web page.

Samsung

  1. Open Settings → Connections → SIM manager.
  2. Tap Add eSIM → Scan QR code.
  3. Follow the prompts, then test a call and mobile data with Wi-Fi off.

Google Pixel

  1. Open Settings → Network & internet → SIMs and tap + (add).
  2. Choose to download a SIM and scan the QR code.

Tip: keep the QR code until the eSIM is working. Some codes only work once, so don't delete the eSIM unless you're sure.

2. Moving everything to a new phone

Do this with both phones charged, side by side, on the same Wi-Fi.

iPhone to iPhone

  1. Turn on the new iPhone and hold it next to the old one.
  2. Quick Start appears on the old phone. Tap Continue and scan the animation with the old phone.
  3. Choose Transfer from iPhone. Apps, photos, messages and settings copy across.

Android to Android

  1. Samsung: use the Smart Switch app on both phones (by cable or wirelessly).
  2. Pixel and most others: during setup, choose Copy apps & data and follow the prompts.

Switching between iPhone and Android

  • Android → iPhone: install Apple's Move to iOS app on the Android phone and choose "Move Data from Android" during iPhone setup.
  • iPhone → Android: use the transfer option shown during the new phone's setup (Samsung's Smart Switch, or Google's Switch to Android app).

Before you hand in or sell the old phone: check your photos, contacts and chats arrived; for WhatsApp, back up your chats first; sign out of iCloud or Google; then reset the old phone to factory settings.

3. "My mobile data isn't working"

Work down this list. Most problems are fixed by step 3.

  1. Check mobile data is on. iPhone: Settings → Mobile Service → Mobile Data. Android: swipe down and check the mobile data tile.
  2. Turn Aeroplane Mode on for 10 seconds, then off. This makes the phone reconnect to the network.
  3. Restart the phone.
  4. Check your data allowance. Many plans don't stop when you run out, they just slow right down, which feels like "no internet". Check usage in your carrier's app.
  5. Check for a network outage in your carrier's app or on their coverage/outage page.
  6. Update the phone. iPhone: Settings → General → Software Update (and accept any carrier settings update). Android: Settings → Software update.
  7. Take the SIM out and put it back in (physical SIM only), and check it isn't damaged.
  8. Reset network settings as a last step. This forgets saved Wi-Fi passwords, so have them ready.
  9. Still not working? Visit a store or contact your carrier. The SIM or account may need checking.

4. Getting your phone ready for an overseas trip

Before you leave

  1. Check your roaming options in your carrier's app. For example, Vodafone's roaming lets you use your normal plan for a daily fee ($5 a day at the time of writing). Check your carrier's current price and which countries are covered.
  2. Make sure roaming is allowed on your account, and add any roaming pack you need.
  3. For long trips, compare a local SIM or travel eSIM. It can be cheaper than paying daily roaming for weeks.

When you land

  1. Turn on Data Roaming. iPhone: Settings → Mobile Service → Mobile Data Options. Android: Settings → Connections (or Network) → Mobile networks.
  2. Use Wi-Fi where you can, and turn off auto-updates and photo backup over mobile data to save your allowance.
  3. Watch for the carrier's welcome text, which confirms your roaming rates.

Tip: if you don't want to roam at all, leave Data Roaming off and use Wi-Fi only. That way you won't get surprise charges.

Menu names can differ slightly between phone models and software versions. Always check your carrier's current roaming prices before travelling.
