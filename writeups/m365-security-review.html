Securing a Small Business's Microsoft 365: A Cyber Essentials-Style Review

Medha Holla · Cloud security and GRC · Tools: Microsoft 365 Business Premium, Entra ID, Exchange Online PowerShell, Defender for Office 365, Intune, Maester

Summary. Most small businesses run on Microsoft 365, and most leave it close to the default settings. I set up a Microsoft 365 Business Premium tenant for a made-up 5-person firm, "Kestrel Accounting Ltd", and reviewed it the way a managed service provider would review a customer.

An open-source scanner, Maester, ran 746 checks from the CIS, CISA SCuBA, EIDSCA and ORCA baselines. Of the 255 that applied, 154 failed. I wrote a customer-style report that maps the gaps to the five Cyber Essentials controls, fixed them in the admin portals and PowerShell, and rescanned the same day: failures fell to 115, passes rose from 101 to 142, and 7 of my 10 findings were verified fixed.

1. The setup

  • Tenant: a Microsoft 365 Business Premium trial with 5 licensed users (a partner, two accountants, an office manager and me as the IT admin).
  • Starting point: a normal new tenant. Microsoft's "security defaults" were on, which force basic MFA, but nothing else had been configured.
  • Scanner: Maester v2.3.0, run from Windows PowerShell with read access to Microsoft Graph, Exchange Online and Teams.
Install-Module Maester, ExchangeOnlineManagement, MicrosoftTeams -Scope CurrentUser -AllowClobber
Install-MaesterTests
Connect-Maester -Service Graph,ExchangeOnline,SecurityCompliance,Teams
Invoke-Maester -OutputFolder "$HOME\Downloads\maester-kestrel\before"

2. Baseline scorecard

I grouped the failures by the five Cyber Essentials controls, because that is the language small UK businesses are assessed in.

1. FirewallsNot assessedA cloud tenant has no boundary firewall; device firewalls are checked once laptops are enrolled.

3. User access controlAmberMFA came only from security defaults, with no Conditional Access, one Global Administrator and no emergency account.

The PowerShell part

Some fixes are faster, or only possible, from Exchange Online PowerShell:

Connect-ExchangeOnline -UserPrincipalName admin@kestrelaccountingltd.onmicrosoft.com
Enable-OrganizationCustomization
Set-AdminAuditLogConfig -UnifiedAuditLogIngestionEnabled $true
Set-ExternalInOutlook -Enabled $true
Get-ManagementRoleAssignment -RoleAssignee "Default Role Assignment Policy" |
  ? { $_.Role -in "My Custom Apps","My Marketplace Apps","My ReadWriteMailbox Apps" } |
  Remove-ManagementRoleAssignment -Confirm:$false
Set-MalwareFilterPolicy -Identity Default -EnableFileFilter $true -FileTypes (...CIS list...)

39 checks went from fail to pass. Many of the 115 that remain are features a 5-person firm would not turn on, such as data loss prevention for US tax numbers or macOS device policies, or the "Strict" email preset, which is meant for a few high-risk users rather than everyone.

5. Problems I hit and how I solved them

  • "Failed to create" on the first Conditional Access policy. A policy can't be switched On while security defaults are still enabled. I turned security defaults off and created the policies straight away, so the tenant was never left without MFA.
  • Microsoft's policy templates exclude the person creating them. That would have left my own admin account outside the MFA rule. I swapped the exclusion to the break-glass account instead. The rescan showed I still need to do this on one policy (Maester check MT.1005).
  • Audit logging command refused to run. Set-AdminAuditLogConfig first needs Enable-OrganizationCustomization, and then takes up to an hour to apply.
  • The Purview portal kept looping between pop-ups, so I turned auditing on from PowerShell instead.
  • The rescan said "not connected". Maester sessions expire, and it must be run from the folder that holds its tests.

6. What I'd tell the client

  • Blocking old Office formats has a business cost. The CIS attachment list includes .doc and .xls. An accounting firm may still receive these from clients, so I would agree the list with them before applying it, or quarantine rather than reject.
  • Keep the break-glass password offline and check twice a year that it still works.
  • Enrol the laptops in Intune next, so BitLocker, attack surface reduction rules and patch status can be enforced and evidenced for Cyber Essentials.

7. What I learned

  • Security defaults are a good start but can't be tailored or audited; Conditional Access gives the same MFA with exceptions you can see and justify.
  • A scanner gives hundreds of results; the useful part of the job is grouping them into a short list a business owner can act on.
  • Rescanning is what turns "I changed a setting" into evidence that the control works.
